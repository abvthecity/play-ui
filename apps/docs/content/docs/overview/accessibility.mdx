---
title: Accessibility
description: How play-ui keeps playful interactions usable by everyone.
---

Motion and gestures are the point of play-ui, so every component is designed to work just as well
without them.

## Keyboard

Every gesture has a keyboard equivalent. In a [card stack](/docs/components/card-stack), the stage is
focusable and the arrow keys swipe the top card; triggers are real `<button>` elements. You can turn
the keys off, or add your own, through props.

## Announcements

Components that change what is on screen can announce the change through a polite live region. You
write the sentence, so it reads naturally in your product's voice. A card stack calls `announce` after
each swipe lands.

## Hidden layers

Parts that are only decoration, such as the cards beneath the top one, their shading, and swipe
indicators, are marked `aria-hidden`, and the card beneath the top one is `inert`, so screen readers and
the tab order only ever see the card in front.

## Reduced motion

By default every component follows the viewer's `prefers-reduced-motion` setting. When reduced motion
is on, a swipe lands at once instead of flying. You can override this per component with the
`reducedMotion` option: `"system"` (the default), `"always"` or `"never"`.

## Your part

Because the components are headless, a few things are yours to get right:

- Give interactive parts a visible focus style.
- Keep enough contrast between text and the colours you choose.
- Give icon-only buttons an accessible name.
